Something that has been true throughout history is that in most fields the learning process is often abstracted from the applied practice. In physics and chemistry you learn and understand simplified models before eventually being told they're just abstractions used to learn fundamental concepts. When learning to sharpen knives you'll often start out with a cheap knife made of soft steel--despite the fact that in practice only harder steels take well to the whetstone. 

LLMs allow people to skip the learning process and move directly to application. They don't force one to slow down and really master foundational concepts. They do not, metaphorically, teach a person to fish.
